Guide · Reporting & insights

The P&L conversation on a Tuesday.

Sales, gross profit, labour — three numbers on one card, updated daily. Not a substitute for your accountant's P&L, but the difference between running the business and being run by it.

Start free trial See the reporting module
What it is

Three lines, live, from the till and the rota.

  • Sales — week-to-date gross takings (net of VAT), from the till or the daily takings log.
  • Gross profit — sales minus cost of goods sold. Every till sale drops the £ of stock consumed at cost; GP is real, not a quarterly estimate.
  • Labour — actual hours worked × hourly rate, from the clock punches.

Optionally, GP% and labour% as ratios against sales, banded green / amber / red against your targets. On the Owner Today screen, all three are on a single card — the first thing you see when you open the app.

Why weekly, not monthly

Speed of decision is the independent's edge.

A chain operator has a regional director watching daily numbers across twenty venues. An independent operator has one shot at the same signal, on the same day the shift finishes — but usually loses it, because the numbers don't arrive until the accountant's monthly P&L three weeks after the fact.

By then, three weeks of rotas have already been built. Three weeks of stock has already been ordered. Three weeks of decisions have been made on gut instead of numbers. That's the tax an independent operator pays for not having a flash view — and it's usually paid in over-rostered Sundays and mis-priced starters.

Flash P&L pulls the P&L conversation forward to Tuesday morning. Not the audited one — the directional one, the one that decides next week's rota and next week's specials. The gap between "we're up on last month" and "we're down 6% on last Tuesday and I know why" is where independent operators start winning.

How to read it

What each line is telling you.

  • Sales up, GP flat. You're taking more money but making the same profit — usually a menu mix problem or a promotion running heavier than intended. Look at Insights → Profit, GP-by-category tab.
  • Sales flat, GP down. Cost is drifting. Suppliers have hiked, waste is up, or a stock leak. Check Movements for waste entries and stocktakes for variance.
  • Sales up, labour % up more. You over-rostered for the volume, or the shifts you added didn't produce the sales you expected. Insights → Labour → sales-per-labour-hour will show which shifts.
  • Sales down, labour % rising sharply. You didn't respond to a soft trading week; last-minute rota cuts would have saved £. This is the pattern to catch on Tuesday morning while there's still Wednesday-Sunday to fix.
What it doesn't tell you

Where flash P&L stops and the accountant's P&L starts.

Flash P&L covers direct trading — sales in, COGS out, labour paid. It does not cover:

  • Rent, rates, utilities, insurance
  • Depreciation, financing costs, corporation tax
  • Bank reconciliation, mis-postings, VAT owed
  • Non-trading income (grants, refunds, deposits)

Those come from your bookkeeper's monthly P&L, and they should. Don't try to make flash P&L do what a proper P&L does; that way lies incorrect operating decisions. Use flash P&L for what it's good at — direction, week-on-week, in time to change something.

The "costs missing" warning

An honest GP figure is better than a flattering one.

A common failure mode of hospitality reporting is displaying a GP figure built on half-populated cost data. If half your products have costs entered and half don't, the reported GP looks great because the uncosted lines have 100% margin. It's a story, not a number.

Insights → Profit surfaces a "costs missing" warning when any product ringing sales has no cost recorded — with a link to the exact product list to fix. It's a small feature that stops a lot of over-confident decisions.
