Guide · Kitchen display

A screen for the kitchen.

Updated 30 October 2026.

Quick answer. A kitchen display for a small pub is a screen that shows orders as they are rung, sent to the station that cooks them. HospitalityApp’s kitchen display is on Complete Plus, £199 a month + VAT per venue, with 50 staff. Printed kitchen tickets, without the screen, are on Complete at £149 a month + VAT. There is no extra software fee per screen.

Kitchen display The till

Who it is for.

This is for a UK pub or small restaurant that plates food and is tired of a paper ticket getting lost between the pass and the fryer. The kitchen needs one shared view of what is coming, and each station needs to see its own work. It is for a venue that already takes the order on HospitalityApp, or is ready to. A screen with nothing feeding it is just a monitor.

A wet-led pub with no kitchen does not need it. Printed tickets are a step before a screen, and they live on the till plan, not on Complete Plus. If you only need a chit on a printer, stay on Complete.

What the screen does.

Orders come in from service: the till on the bar, or a server’s phone. Mobile ordering, taking the order at the table on a phone, is on the same plan as the kitchen display. Tickets are routed to the relevant station, so the grill and the pass are not reading each other’s work. The pass stays informed as the shift gets busier. The point of the screen is a clearer next step, not a second till.

The till itself runs on an iPad, an Android tablet, or a Windows 10 or 11 PC, including a touchscreen EPOS terminal you already own, with no per-terminal software fee. The kitchen display is software on Complete Plus. We do not publish a separate hardware price for the screen, because the software does not charge one. Use a screen you are happy to put in a kitchen.

On a small brigade the value is fewer shouted orders. The person on the pass can see what is fired, and the station only sees its own ticket. That is the whole job of the screen. It does not replace a head chef’s judgement, and it does not cost the menu. When the service is finished, the same sales sit in stock and in the insights suite, which are on Complete, so the screen and the gross-profit review are not two products from two suppliers.

How it relates to the till.

Complete, at £149 a month + VAT per venue with 30 staff, includes the till, stock, gross profit, tips and tronc, the insights suite, and printed kitchen tickets. Complete Plus, at £199 a month + VAT with 50 staff, adds four things that are not on Complete: mobile ordering, the kitchen display, table reservations and floor plans. None of those four is included if you stop at Complete.

Essentials at £49 and Professional at £99 do not include the till. A kitchen display with no till has nothing to show, so the practical starting point for a food pub is Complete, and the screen is the step up to Complete Plus. Annual billing is £1,490 for Complete and £1,990 for Complete Plus, paid up front, which saves two months. A 30-day free trial of Complete Plus needs no card, so you can see the screen before you choose the plan.

What we do not claim.

HospitalityApp does not take the card payment on the till. You record the payment method. Your own card terminal takes the money. If you need to tap a card on the same device that rang the food, a kitchen display does not close that gap. That is a real limit, and it is described on the pub till comparison and the Square and Lightspeed pages.

Ingredient-level recipe costing is not the reason to buy this screen. Stock and gross profit, on Complete, track what sold and the profit behind it. They are not a recipe-engineering suite. If that is the main problem you are solving, say so before you buy a display.

Questions people ask.

Which plan includes the kitchen display?

Complete Plus, at £199 a month + VAT per venue, with 50 staff. It also includes mobile ordering, table reservations and floor plans. Printed kitchen tickets, without the screen, are on Complete at £149 a month + VAT.

Is there a fee per kitchen screen?

No per-terminal software fee. The kitchen display is included on Complete Plus rather than priced per screen. The till on Complete has the same rule: a second till does not add a software licence.

Where do the orders come from?

From the till, and from mobile ordering on a staff phone. Mobile ordering is on Complete Plus, the same plan as the screen. Tickets are routed to the station that needs them.

Do I need it if the kitchen is small?

Only if a shared screen is clearer than a printed ticket. A small kitchen can stay on Complete and print tickets. The screen is the step up, not a requirement for serving food.
