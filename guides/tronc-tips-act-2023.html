Guide · Tips & tronc

The Tips Act 2023, in plain English.

What the Act requires, what a compliant written policy looks like, and the durable record a tribunal (or your bar staff) can actually ask for. For UK hospitality operators, not lawyers.

What the Act does

Three obligations, from 1 October 2024.

The Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 places three legal duties on any UK employer whose workers receive tips, gratuities or service charge:

  • Pass 100% of tips to workers. No deductions for card processing, admin fees, breakage or "shrinkage recovery". The only lawful deduction is income tax that HMRC requires you to operate under PAYE (which a properly-constituted tronc scheme avoids on National Insurance).
  • Allocate fairly and transparently, in a written policy. The allocation model must be defined, consistent, and defensible. It has to be given to workers on hire and available on request. "We decide each week" is not a policy.
  • Keep a durable record for three years. A dated, itemised record of every tip received and every allocation made. Workers can request their own allocation records; you must produce them within four weeks.
Fair and transparent

What the Code of Practice actually lets you consider.

The statutory Code of Practice (published by the Department for Business and Trade) lists the factors an employer may reasonably use when designing an allocation model:

  • Type of role. Front-of-house, back-of-house, kitchen, bar. It is lawful to weight roles differently — many venues weight FOH higher because tips are customer-facing — provided the weighting is written down and applied consistently.
  • Hours worked. The commonest fair basis. Weekly tips divided by weekly hours-worked, weighted by role if that's your policy.
  • Customer intention. A tip attached to a specific table stays with that section; a tip added to a group service charge goes to the pool. Your policy has to say which is which.
  • Seniority and length of service. Allowable but risky if applied inconsistently — new starters must not be zeroed out for months on end.
  • Performance and disciplinary status. Only where there's a written, applied performance framework. Ad-hoc "reward the good ones" allocations don't survive scrutiny.

What you cannot do: penalise casual workers, agency workers or new starters for their status; withhold tips as a disciplinary sanction outside a formal process; or vary the model unpredictably between weeks.

The written policy

What has to be on the page.

  • Whether tips are pooled (a "tronc") or distributed by seat / section.
  • The allocation model — hours-based, role-weighted, or a defined combination.
  • How cash tips are handled (many venues pool cash into the tronc; some allow retention with a declared amount).
  • Frequency of allocation — weekly is the industry standard.
  • How disputes are raised and resolved.
  • How workers request their own allocation records, and the four-week response window.
  • Who administers the tronc (a designated troncmaster is required if you want the NIC saving that a properly-constituted tronc can attract).

Give a copy to every worker on hire; make the current version available in a place workers can access; date and version every revision.

The record

What a compliant weekly tronc statement contains.

  • Period — dates of the week (or pay period).
  • Pool total — card tips + cash tips + service charge, broken out.
  • Allocation model named — "Hours worked, FOH weighted 1.2x".
  • Per-person allocation — worker name, hours worked (or applicable weight), allocation £, running total for the period.
  • Adjustments and notes — any manual override, with the reason.
  • Draft / Finalised badge — the point at which the calculation was closed.

Retention: three years. A worker can request their own records for the past three years; you must provide them within four weeks.

Penalties

What a tribunal can order.

Enforcement is through employment tribunals. A worker (or ex-worker within 12 months) can bring a claim. If upheld, the tribunal can order:

  • Back-payment of any withheld tips.
  • Compensation of up to £5,000 per worker.
  • A public revision of your allocation policy.

The tribunal isn't testing your intent — it's testing whether your written policy is compliant and whether your record shows the policy was followed. The two artefacts (policy + weekly statement) are the case.
