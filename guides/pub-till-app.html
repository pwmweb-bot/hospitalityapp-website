What it is

Pub till app, defined.

A pub till app is point-of-sale software designed for the rhythms of pub work — a drinks-led menu with half-pint and pint pricing on the same card, fast counter service, a sports-event mode that flips the layout for fixture days, and licensing records like the refusal register kept in the same system as the till rather than in a paper book behind the bar. It runs on an iPad or Android tablet behind the bar, replacing legacy EPOS terminals that were built for retail and grafted onto hospitality afterwards.

The difference matters. A retail till with a beer skin doesn't know that a half is half the price of a pint, that Cask Marque audits expect a cellar log, that Friday-night service collapses if the second bartender has to navigate three menus to pour a round.

The seven features

What a pub till should do.

Use this as the checklist when you're shopping. Anything missing three or more wasn't built for the trade.

1 · Half + pint on one card

The fundamental pub-till test. Both prices visible, both tappable, both feeding the same product code. Not a sub-menu, not a modifier, not a separate SKU.

2 · Drinks-led category order

Draught first. Bottles & cans second. Wine, spirits, cocktails, mixers, then food. Match the bar's brain; staff find what they need in under a second.

3 · Licensing records in the same system

Refusal register, Challenge 25 refreshers, DPS details — logged in the app rather than a paper book behind the bar. Test-purchase season stops being a scramble for a folder.

4 · Cellar log alongside the till

Daily line-clean, gas level, cellar temp — kept in the same app as the till, not on a clipboard by the stillage. The brewery rep walks in, you tap export, hand them the log.

5 · Sports-event mode

Six Nations Saturday flips the layout — bigger touch targets, tab strip for grouped rounds, faster categories. The Champions League final isn't the night to discover your till's limits.

6 · Card-processor agnostic

Use Stripe, SumUp, Square, Zettle or your existing standalone terminal. The till records the sale; your card machine takes the card. Keep your existing rate.

7 · Takings feed labour cost

Every pint sold updates today's takings, which updates today's labour-cost %. Spot the Wednesday wobble at 3pm, not at month-end.

A Friday night, scripted

What service looks like on the till.

17:00 · Pre-shift open

Duty manager works the opening checks in the app — fridge temps, cellar gas, line clean if due — each photographed, each logged. Bar staff clock in on their phones by geofence, QR or NFC. Then the till goes full-screen for service, and every sale from here carries the name of whoever entered their PIN.

19:30 · Two-deep at the bar

Half of Guinness, pint of Neck Oil — three taps. The half/pint card means no fumbling. Payment goes through the pub's own SumUp terminal — the till records it as a card sale and stays out of the card contract. Total updates the night's takings in real time. Bartender moves to the next customer.

21:00 · ID check, then refused sale

Customer orders. Bartender asks for ID. ID looks off. Sale refused, customer leaves, bartender moves on. The register entry is made in the app under Alcohol licensing — on a phone or the back-office tablet, not on the till screen mid-service — so the licensing file builds itself over the season instead of being reconstructed the week before a review.

23:00 · Last orders, then close

Tab strip across the top shows open tabs. Bartender closes each one. Final Z-read run from the till. Takings figure auto-populates today's total. Closing checklist is ticked off in the app: kitchen off, music off, bins out, alarm armed — each photographed.

Cost vs traditional EPOS

What a tied pub pays vs an independent.

Brewery-supplied EPOS

Often "free" with a tied agreement. Real cost: locked into the brewery's stocklist, the brewery's card processor, the brewery's reporting schedule. Renegotiating the tie means migrating tills.

Standalone pub EPOS

£45–£89/mo software + £400–£1,200 per terminal + integration fees. Decent if you have three tills and a head office. Heavy overhead for a single-site free house.
