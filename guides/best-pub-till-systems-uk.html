Guide · Till / EPOS · August 2026

The best pub till systems in the UK, compared on what they actually cost.

Eleven EPOS systems sold to UK pubs and bars, checked against the vendor's own live pages and, where they publish one, their actual contract. Every figure is either sourced or marked not published — we do not repeat prices from review sites. Written by a competitor, which is exactly why the numbers about everyone else are held to a stricter standard than the ones about us.

How to read this page

Vendor means we read it on the vendor's own live page or in their own published contract, and quote it. Reported means a third party published it, the source is named, and the vendor does not confirm it. Not published means nobody publishes it — and that is a finding, not a gap.

All figures observed 25 August 2026. Prices change, promotions expire and vendors repackage. We re-verify this page every quarter — next review November 2026. If a figure about your company is wrong, tell us and we will correct it and note the correction.

Start here

Three questions that change the answer more than the headline price.

Almost every comparison of till systems compares the monthly software fee. For a pub, that number is very often the least important of the four you are actually committing to.

  1. What does a second till cost? A pub with a lounge bar and a public bar needs two. That is free with some vendors and £39 a month — the price of an entire entry plan — with others. One vendor's own terms make the licence chargeable per terminal without publishing the amount.
  2. What are you actually signing? Four of the eleven set the contract length in an order form they do not publish, auto-renew it, and charge out the remainder of the term if you leave. One vendor's marketing page and its own master services agreement flatly contradict each other.
  3. Who owns your card processing? If the till and the payments are one product, changing acquirer means changing till. On £60,000 a month of card turnover, a rate you cannot shop is worth far more than any software fee on this page.

The tables below lead on those three, because those three are where the money is.

The eleven, in detail

What each one is actually good at.

Ordered by usefulness to a single-site independent, not alphabetically and not by our convenience.

01 — priceable today

Tabology

The closest like-for-like alternative for a single-site independent pub, and the most transparent vendor we looked at: it publishes all three tier prices and every per-device extra, which almost nobody else in UK pub EPOS does. That transparency also exposes the trap. The £39 headline is a one-till price, and each additional till is another £39 — the same as the whole entry plan. Staff scheduling is not in the £39 tier either, so a pub wanting a till and a rota starts at £59, and a two-bar pub with a kitchen screen is at £109 before VAT is settled. There is no compliance capability, and payroll is an export rather than a payroll run.

Best forA one- or two-till wet-led independent that already owns an iPad, wants till plus stock plus rota from one supplier under £110 a month, and wants to be able to walk away.
  • Additional terminal £39/mo. Mini EPOS (iPhone) £19. Kitchen Display Screen £11. iPad menu £14. Vendor
  • Hardware outright: starter pack £357, or £734.50 with an iPad. Bring-your-own is explicit.
  • Our arithmetic: Pro £59 + second till £39 + one kitchen screen £11 = £109/month, VAT position unstated.
  • Setup or onboarding fee is not published. “Full onboarding” appears only at the £99 tier — worth asking about.
02 — priceable today

Square for Restaurants

The strongest pure-cost answer at the bottom of this market, and we should say so plainly: a genuinely working till at £0 a month, unlimited devices at every tier, no per-till fee, no minimum term, and a full UK price list in pounds without a sales call. Its economics invert as you grow, because the flat 1.75% is the dominant cost line for any busy site and there is no way to take a better acquiring deal without replacing the till too. The advertised price is also not the working price — a realistic single-site build with rota and ingredient-level stock lands nearer £169 a month than £69.

Best forA new or small owner-operated café, bar or coffee shop, roughly under £30–40k a month in card turnover, that wants to be trading this afternoon on an iPad it already owns.
  • UK card rates: 1.75% in person, 1.4% + 25p online, 2.5% keyed. Vendor
  • Add-ons: Kiosk £35/device, MarketMan inventory £79, Shifts £3/head after 5 free, Loyalty from £25.
  • Our arithmetic: Plus £69 + Shifts for 12 staff (£21) + MarketMan £79 = from £169/month ex VAT. A site doing £60,000/month on card pays roughly £1,050/month in processing.
  • A conflict on their own site: the general UK pricing page shows Plus at “£29+”, the restaurants page says £69 per location.
  • Square KDS is Android only; Square's own help centre tells existing iPad users they must migrate hardware.
03 — priceable today

SumUp POS Pro (formerly Goodtill)

Credit for two unusual things: it publishes a real software price with a VAT footnote, and it explicitly lets you connect a card reader of your choice rather than forcing its own acquiring. Against that sit two problems. The £49 headline is not what a pub pays — the Hospitality module is £19 and the Kitchen Display Screen is £19 per terminal, so a realistic configuration is £87 a month excluding VAT. And the marketing and the contract disagree.

Best forAn independent café, bar or small restaurant that wants a mature hospitality till at a knowable software price and wants to keep its card acquiring negotiable.
  • The contract conflict, published as a conflict. Pricing page: “you'll never be tied down by long-term contracts.” Their own Master Services Agreement, clause 8.1: terms “automatically renew for successive one (1) year periods” escapable only on “ninety (90) days' written notice”, with all fees “non-cancellable and non-refundable”. Get the term confirmed in writing.
  • Six modules at £19/mo each. The KDS line is scoped per terminal; the £49 base is not scoped at all — so a two-till pub must confirm what £49 buys.
  • Our arithmetic: £49 + Hospitality £19 + one KDS £19 = £87/month ex VAT, about 78% above the headline.
04 — priceable today

Kobas

The only vendor here that seriously competes with us on scope rather than price. Clocking in and task management are in the £99 base tier, and the platform genuinely spans rota, HR, holidays, stock ordering, recipe engineering, allergen profiling and compliance tasks. Two things temper it: the entry price is £99 with all five functional modules — including the rota — as paid add-ons whose prices are not published anywhere, so a pub wanting till plus rota plus stock cannot cost Kobas from the website at all. The genuinely all-inclusive tier is £369. Support at £99 is knowledge base and AI chat; a human team starts at £250.

Best forAn ambitious independent or a 2–5 site group that has decided to run rota, stock, HR and compliance from the same system as the till, and can justify £250–£369 a month.
  • No per-terminal fee: “no per-user or per-device licence fees”. A real strength for a multi-till pub.
  • A realistic configuration price is not calculable from published information — the module prices simply are not there.
  • Card rates: Kobas publishes examples only, and two different ones on the same page — 0.3% + 3p and 0.4% + 2p.
05 — priceable today

Lightspeed Restaurant

Publishes its tier prices and, to its credit, the per-extra-register fee that most of this market hides. Read the tiers carefully though: a “licence” is a device slot, so on Basic £79 a single till consumes your only licence, and Tableside, Kitchen Display, QR ordering and Inventory are all paid add-ons at prices the site never publishes. Extra registers are £39 a month each, forever. The transparency stops precisely where the cost stacking begins — and it stops entirely at payments.

Best forA food-led restaurant or gastropub wanting polished tableside, kitchen display and ingredient-level stock, that will buy rota, payroll and compliance separately.
  • The feature-by-tier table contains no row for rota, scheduling, payroll, time clock or compliance at any tier.
  • Early termination charges unpaid non-recurring fees plus all recurring fees for the remainder of the term, plus the difference between list price and your discounted price. Vendor
  • No hardware prices are published. Bring-your-own is qualified: “in some cases you might be able to use your existing hardware.”
06 — sales-led

Epos Now

The vendor most likely to have already telephoned you, and the most useful thing we can do is make its cost structure legible. Its UK pricing page carries no tiers, no plan names and no monthly figure beyond a single asterisked footnote. On the same day, its own live site published three different card processing rates on three different pages. Its own terms make the software licence chargeable per terminal, run the licence on a 12-month auto-renewing clock and the care plan on a 36-month one, and on any £0-upfront deal keep legal title to the hardware. Against all that: a real UK company at real scale, 4.3/5 from 26,270 Trustpilot reviews, a genuinely good stock module for wet-led sites, and one contractual protection most rivals do not offer.

Best forA price-sensitive single-site independent wanting the cheapest cash cost to start trading, content to process cards through Epos Now, prepared to negotiate hard and diary every cancellation window.
  • The rate conflict, as observed on 25 August 2026: the Payment Processing page said “from 0.8%”, the Payments Lite page said “1.5% fixed”, and the Tablet POS page said “1.70% flat rate”.
  • Their own terms: “The annual Software licence fee is chargeable for each separate point of sale terminal.”
  • Care Plan is a fixed 36-month contract, auto-renewing for another 36, with each additional solution on its own separate 36-month term.
  • Minimum Transaction Level: per rolling 28 days, at least 75% of your card processing or £2,000.
  • In their favour: clause 4.3 permits termination on one month's notice if a price rise exceeds CPI + 2%. Few rivals offer that.
  • Full head-to-head: we have a dedicated Epos Now comparison covering the five contract clauses worth checking before you sign.
  • Bundle promotions carry countdown timers and drift — check the price on the day. Terms cited are Epos Now's own, as filed to the UK Government's G-Cloud 14 framework, dated 3 May 2024.
07 — sales-led

Toast UK

Toast publishes a starting figure at all, which puts it ahead of most of the sales-led field, and it publishes its full UK merchant agreement, which puts it ahead of nearly everyone. The agreement is the problem. The Initial Term's length is set in the order form and never published, it auto-renews for successive one-year periods, fees are non-cancellable, and leaving early costs the remaining software fees for the term. You are locked to Toast's designated payment provider — currently Adyen — which Toast may change “with or without notice”. Against that: 24/7 support, real offline operation, and genuine UK pub references.

Best forA food-led gastropub or small group with real kitchen complexity, handhelds and online ordering, replacing four suppliers at once, comfortable committing to a multi-year term and to Adyen.
  • Both published prices are floors — the comparison chart labels the Essentials row “Starting at” too.
  • Toast “advise[s] against using your own hardware” and is “unable to support equipment not purchased through Toast.”
  • We do not publish a Toast card rate. A specific figure circulates widely on third-party sites; it is traceable to nothing on Toast's own UK site, so it does not appear here.
08 — sales-led

Zonal — and Akru

Treating “Zonal” as one comparable thing for this audience would be misleading. Zonal's own Pubs and Bars page targets “large, multi-site pub and bar operators” and then carries a separate line — “Independent operator? We've got you” — linking away to Akru, a Zonal-owned but separately branded cloud POS. So the product a single-site independent will actually be offered is Akru, not Aztec.

Akru is unusually candid: it publishes one flat card rate, says the software is free, and says there is no contract lock-in. The catch is structural. Because effectively all of Akru's revenue comes from card processing, the cost scales directly with your takings, there is no cap, and you cannot take the free till and keep your existing acquirer. Akru is also till-only — no rota, no clock-in, no payroll, no stock, not even the stock control that is Aztec's best feature.

Best forAztec — managed and multi-site groups from roughly ten sites up, and pubs with rooms needing a real PMS. Akru — a single-site independent on Android tablets with separate answers already for rota, payroll and compliance.
  • The arithmetic that matters: on £40,000 a month of card turnover at an £18 average transaction, Akru's published rates come to roughly £423 a month. “No POS fees” is not the same as cheaper — model it against your own turnover.
  • Akru runs on “any Android device, version 13 and above”. Android only — an iPad-equipped venue cannot reuse its tablets.
  • Aztec's named customers on Zonal's own EPoS page include Wetherspoons, Stonegate, Greene King and Pizza Express. This is genuinely an estate system.
09 — the incumbent

ICRTouch (TouchPoint)

Very likely what you are switching from, and it cannot be compared on price, because there is no ICRTouch price. It writes software and sells nothing direct: every sale, install and support call goes through a local Authorised Partner, and its own terms push the commercial relationship downstream. What it does publish is where the labour gap sits, and it is unusually explicit about it. Its genuine strength for a wet-led pub is that TouchPoint is a local application rather than a browser tab, so a broadband outage does not stop service — and the free choice of six integrated card processors means you can re-tender your merchant rate without replacing the till.

Best forA busy wet-led pub that needs the till to keep ringing when the internet drops, has the cash for a four-figure per-terminal outlay, and has a local dealer it trusts.
  • The rota gap, verbatim: “All staff management integrations require TouchOffice Web+”, with rota supplied by S4labour, Rotaready, Workforce or Shiftie. The Web+ price is not published anywhere — and it gates every rota integration.
  • Resellers price TouchPoint as a product rather than a subscription, which is consistent with a one-off licence, but ICRTouch publishes no licensing model. Confirm with a dealer in writing.
  • Per-terminal licensing is not published — ask whether a second till needs a second licence.
  • ICRTouch's own site gives two different installed-base figures — “over 180,000 UK installs” on one page and “over 150,000” on another. Either way, a very large UK installed base.
10 — correcting the record

TouchBistro

A strong, restaurant-native iPad POS — for North America. From a UK connection on 25 August 2026, the GB path redirects to the US homepage, the UK pricing path returns a 404, and the site serves USD pricing anyway. There is no pound sign anywhere on it. More decisively: TouchBistro Payments is “currently available for our U.S customers only”, and Labor Management, Inventory, KDS, Loyalty and Online Ordering are all published as available only in Canada and the continental United States.

Best forA US or Canadian full-service restaurant. For a UK independent, only a table-service venue that specifically wants this till and will run it as a till and nothing more.
  • We include it mainly to correct the record. Several UK comparison sites publish a GBP tier table for TouchBistro — commonly £59, £50 and £40 per licence. It is unsourced, appears nowhere on TouchBistro's own site, and does not match their current plan structure. Do not budget from it.
  • Billed by a Toronto company under Ontario law, with arbitration seated in Toronto.

Methodology

How we compared these systems.

Between 20 and 25 August 2026 we researched eleven point-of-sale products sold to UK hospitality businesses, plus our own. For each one we fetched the vendor's own live UK pages — pricing, features, hardware, payments — and, wherever the vendor publishes one, its actual contract: terms of service, master services agreement, merchant agreement or filed trading terms.

Every figure was then independently re-checked by a second reviewer against the source page or document, and anything that could not be reproduced was removed. Where a vendor publishes no price, we say “not published” rather than repeating a figure from a review site. Where a figure comes from a third party, it is marked as reported and the source is named and dated. Where a vendor's own pages contradict each other — and three of them do — we publish the contradiction rather than choosing the more convenient number. Where a vendor does not state whether prices include VAT, we say so, because that is a 20% difference. We show our arithmetic whenever we build a worked example, so you can check it.

We are not neutral. HospitalityApp is our product and this page exists to sell it. That is exactly why the numbers about our competitors are held to a stricter standard than the ones about us, why our own weaknesses are listed under our own name, and why there is a table above telling you when to buy from someone else.

Prices change, promotions expire and vendors repackage their plans. Every figure here carries the date we observed it, and we re-verify the whole page every quarter — next scheduled review November 2026. If you believe a figure about your company is wrong or out of date, email us and we will correct it and note the correction.

Also checked and deliberately excluded: Access EPoS, TISSL, Centegra, Guestline and Vita Mojo. All publish no pricing and are built for multi-site estates; there was nothing we could say about them to the standard above beyond “no price published”, which does not earn a row. We name them here rather than omitting them silently.

Common questions

What buyers ask us.

Why do so many EPOS companies not publish a price?

Because the money is usually not in the software. Several vendors here make most of their margin on card processing, on a care-and-support subscription, or on hardware — and a published software price would invite you to compare the wrong number. Of the twelve products we looked at, four publish a complete price list you can budget from, two publish a starting figure only, and the rest publish nothing at all. If you cannot find a price, that is a commercial decision rather than an oversight, and it means the first number you see will have been chosen for you.

What does a second till actually cost?

This is the question that changes the answer most, and headline prices are worst at it. With HospitalityApp, Square and Kobas, a second till adds nothing to the software bill. With Tabology it is £39 a month — the same as their entire entry plan. With Lightspeed it is £39. Toast's own footnote says additional charges apply without saying what they are. Epos Now's own terms make the licence chargeable per terminal. ICRTouch publishes no answer at all. If you run two bars, ask this before you compare anything else.

“No contract” — what does that actually mean?

It depends whether it is on a marketing page or in a contract. Square publishes that no long-term contract is required. Akru and Tabology make similar website claims, but we could not find terms documents for either, so we report them as site statements. SumUp is the sharpest example: its pricing page promises you will never be tied to long-term contracts, while the master services agreement it publishes sets auto-renewing one-year terms escapable only on ninety days' notice. Lightspeed, Toast and TouchBistro all leave the length to an order form you sign, all auto-renew, and all charge out the remainder if you leave early. Epos Now runs two clocks at once — twelve months on the licence, thirty-six on the care plan. Ask for the term in writing, ask the notice period, and diary the cancellation window the day you sign.

Do I have to use the till company's card processing, and does it matter?

With Square, Lightspeed, Toast and Akru, effectively yes — the payments and the till are the same product, so changing acquirer means changing till. With HospitalityApp, SumUp POS Pro, Kobas, Tabology and ICRTouch, no. Whether it matters depends entirely on your card volume. A quiet café doing £8,000 a month is unlikely to beat Square's 1.75%, and the convenience is worth more than the margin. A busy pub doing £60,000 a month is paying roughly £1,050 a month in processing at that rate — against a £69 software fee — and cannot shop it. Do that sum against your own takings before you read “free software” as “cheaper”.

Can I use the iPad or tablet I already own?

Often, but check the kitchen as well as the bar. Tabology says outright you can convert any iPad. Square's till runs on an iPad you own — but Square's kitchen display is Android-only and iOS support has been retired, so an all-iPad venue has to buy a screen. Akru runs on Android 13+ and only Android. Lightspeed says “in some cases”, which is a qualified maybe. Toast advises against your own hardware and will not support it. Full ICRTouch TouchPoint is a Windows application on a dealer-supplied terminal, so an iPad is no use at all. HospitalityApp runs on hardware you already own.

Why doesn't HospitalityApp do integrated card payments yet?

It should stop you buying us if tapping a card on the same device that rang the sale is important to how you trade — and if it is, Square, SumUp, Toast or Lightspeed will serve you better than we will. We record the payment method; your own terminal takes the money. Our Dojo integration is blocked on ISV access, which is not in our gift to resolve on a timetable. The trade-off is that you keep your own acquiring relationship and your own negotiated rate, and you are not choosing your card processor by choosing a till. We would rather tell you that here than after you have signed.
