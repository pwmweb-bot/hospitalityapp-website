What it is

Staff communication app, defined.

A staff communication app is where your team gets the message that matters — an announcement, a direct question, a request to cover Friday — in one place, tied to the same rota and staff records the manager already uses. It's not a general group chat that happens to have your staff in it.

The distinction matters because most venue messages are really about the rota: who's on, who can swap, who saw the new policy. When the message and the schedule live in different apps, someone has to be the bridge between them — re-typing a chat agreement into the rota, chasing who's actually read the allergen update. A staff communication app removes the bridge.

The checklist

Seven things staff comms must do.

1 · Announcements with read status

Post once; see who's actually seen it. A changed opening time shouldn't rely on hoping people scrolled.

2 · Direct messages

One-to-one and small groups, inside the same app as the rota — not on personal WhatsApp.

3 · Shift swaps that become shifts

Staff request a swap, manager approves in one tap, the rota updates. No chat thread to re-key.

4 · Tied to the rota

"Can anyone do Friday?" goes to the people who could actually work it — because the app knows the schedule.

5 · Leavers drop automatically

End someone's employment and they leave the conversation. No ex-staff lingering in the group chat.

6 · Push notifications

Rota published, shift changed, announcement posted — the phone tells the team, so nobody misses a start.

7 · Work stays at work

Staff keep their personal numbers private and their evenings their own. The app closes when the shift ends.

The hidden cost

WhatsApp is quietly costing you shifts.

The group chat feels free, and it works — right up until the Friday nobody covered because the message got buried under twelve others, or the new starter who never got added, or the ex-bartender who's still reading everything. There are no read receipts you can act on, no link to the rota, and no way to tell "seen it" from "scrolled past it". A staff communication app makes the message accountable: posted, seen, actioned.

"The team likes WhatsApp though."

They like fast and familiar — which a good staff app also is, on the phone they already carry. What they don't like is work leaking into their evening and their number being on a list. The app gives the speed without the leak.

"What about read receipts?"

Announcements show who's seen them, so a manager knows the allergen change or the shift move has actually landed — the one thing a group chat can't reliably tell you.

"Does it replace all messaging?"

It covers the work: announcements, shift comms, swaps, one-to-one questions. Where it earns its place is that all of that is tied to the rota, not floating in a separate app.

FAQ

Staff communication apps, asked & answered.

What is a staff communication app?

Where the team gets announcements, direct messages and shift updates in one place, tied to the same rota and staff records the manager uses — not a separate group chat.

Why not just use WhatsApp?

It mixes work with personal life, has no reliable read receipts, keeps ex-staff in the group, and lives nowhere near the rota. A purpose-built app fixes all four.

Should comms connect to the rota?

Yes — most messages are about shifts. When comms and the rota are one app, a swap request becomes an approved shift in a tap.

Can managers see who read an announcement?

Yes — announcements show read status, so you know a policy or a time change actually landed with the team.
