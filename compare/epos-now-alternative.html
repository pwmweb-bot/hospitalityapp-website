An Epos Now alternative — and when they are the better buy.

Epos Now is the vendor most likely to have already phoned you. This page is not a hit piece: it starts with what they are genuinely good at, then walks through the five clauses in their own published terms worth understanding before you sign, and ends by naming the situations where you should buy from them rather than from us.

Before anything else

Epos Now is a real product from a real UK company at real scale. As at 25 August 2026 they hold 4.3 out of 5 from 26,270 Trustpilot reviews, with 83% at five stars. They offer UK phone support and on-site engineers, more than a hundred integrations, and a stock module that genuinely goes deeper than most at this price point — particularly for wet-led sites.

Nothing on this page argues the product does not work. The argument is about contract structure and pricing transparency, which are a different question from software quality, and which happen to be the questions that cost independent operators money.

We also compete with them, so read this accordingly. Every factual claim below is sourced to something Epos Now published themselves, and dated.

The short version

Which of us you should buy.

Buy Epos Now if you need integrated card payments on the device that rings the sale; you want a UK engineer who physically turns up; you need a large integration catalogue; deep stock and recipe costing is your primary problem; or vendor longevity and thousands of public reviews are how you judge risk. All five are real, and we lose every one of them.

Buy us if the second till costing nothing matters; if you want to keep your own card processor without paying a premium for the privilege; if you want the rota, payroll and compliance in the same system rather than bolted on; or if you would rather not sign a 36-month clock alongside a 12-month one.

Everything below is the evidence for those two paragraphs.

The contract

Five things worth understanding before you sign.

These come from Epos Now's own UK terms and conditions as filed to the UK Government's G-Cloud 14 framework and dated 3 May 2024, and from their live site on 25 August 2026. Terms change — ask for the current version and check these clauses in it rather than taking our word for it.

1. There is no published price list

As at 25 August 2026 their UK pricing page carried no tiers, no plan names and no monthly figure at all. The only number on it was a footnote:

“Discounted price for the Complete Solution bundle requires a 12-month payments, care and support subscription starting at £54 per month.” Epos Now UK pricing page, observed 25 August 2026

Hardware bundles were advertised the same day between roughly £149 and £349, each with a countdown timer. Those drift, so check the price on the day rather than trusting any figure — including ours.

Why it matters: a vendor that does not publish pricing is not disorganised. It is a commercial decision, and it means the first number you see will have been chosen after they know your turnover. Ask for total contract value — monthly fee times the full term, plus setup, plus support — not a monthly figure.

2. The licence is chargeable per terminal

“The annual Software licence fee is chargeable for each separate point of sale terminal… Each licence does not allow the Software to exist on more than one point of sale terminal.” Epos Now UK terms and conditions, dated 3 May 2024

We are deliberately not publishing a current per-terminal amount. The price list inside that filed document is dated May 2024 and is demonstrably out of date against their live site — it prices a kitchen display at a figure their own website no longer shows. The principle of per-terminal charging is published and current; the amount is not, so we will not guess at it.

Why it matters: a two-bar pub with a kitchen screen has three screens. Ask them to quote in writing for the exact number of tills, kitchen displays, handhelds and back-office logins you need, and what a fifth till costs in year two.

With HospitalityApp a second till costs £0 in software. Square and Kobas also charge nothing for extra devices — this is not unique to us.

3. Two contract clocks, running at different speeds

The software licence and the care plan are separate agreements on separate terms:

  • Software licence: minimum 12 months, auto-renewing for further 12-month periods, cancellable only on written notice at least 30 days before the next renewal.
  • ProtectNow Care Plan: charged per month per solution on a fixed 36-month contract, auto-renewing for another 36 months — and each additional solution is covered by its own separate 36-month term.
  • Early exit from a fixed period: the aggregate of the remaining monthly payments, with a 3.5% discount for paying it.

Why it matters: two clocks means two cancellation windows, and the longer one is three years. Add a second till in year two and you start a fresh 36-month term on that solution alone. Ask for both end dates and both notice periods on the order confirmation, and diary them the day you sign.

4. Keeping your own bank has a price

Their terms provide for a Non-Integrated Payment Premium of £29.00 per month per Pro Solution where integrated payments are not activated within 60 days. Plus and Base customers face a different structure — £14.00 per solution, or a £25.00 per month Standard Support Plan.

There is also a Minimum Transaction Level: per rolling 28 days, at least 75% of your card processing or £2,000.

Why it matters: the lock-in is economic rather than technical. You can keep your own acquirer; it costs £348 a year per Pro solution to do it. On a pub turning over £600,000 a year on cards, three tenths of a percentage point is £1,800 a year — so whether you can shop your rate is worth more than the software fee either way.

We charge nothing for keeping your own processor, because we have no payments product to protect. That is also why we have no integrated payments at all — see below.

5. On £0-upfront deals, you do not own the hardware

“Legal title to the hardware remains with us.” Epos Now UK terms and conditions, dated 3 May 2024

Hardware must be returned on termination, and unreturned kit can be invoiced at £1,500 (Plus) or £1,200 (Basic) after 60 days. Complimentary implementation training is “subject to availability, which will consist of up to 4 hours”, with anything beyond quoted on application.

Why it matters: “free” hardware on a £0-upfront plan is a bailment, not a gift. That is a normal and legitimate commercial structure — but it is worth knowing before you treat the terminal as an asset, and worth asking exactly how many days you have to return it.

And one clause in their favour

Clause 4.3 permits you to terminate on one month's notice if a price rise, or the aggregate of rises over a rolling 12 months, exceeds CPI + 2%.

That is a genuine protection and most of their rivals do not offer it, including us. If you are worried about mid-contract price increases, it is a real point in their favour and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

The rate question

Three different card rates, on their own site, on the same day.

On 25 August 2026 we read three different headline card-processing rates across three Epos Now pages:

  • The Payment Processing page said “we charge from 0.8%”
  • The Payments Lite page said “1.5% fixed” on Visa, Mastercard, Diners and Discover, with an opt-in 2.5% for American Express, JCB and UnionPay
  • The Tablet POS page said “1.70% flat rate”

We are not suggesting any of those is untrue — “from 0.8%” and a fixed 1.5% can both be accurate about different products or different volumes. But they cannot all be your rate, and you will not find out which applies to you from the website.

Their card processing runs on a 12-month contract, then monthly rolling. The Payments Lite terminal is £179 + VAT outright, or £15 + VAT a month on a minimum 12-month rental.

What to do about it: get your rate in writing, with every recurring line on the merchant statement listed — not just the headline percentage, but PCI fee, PCI non-compliance fee, minimum monthly service charge, authorisation fee, terminal rental and gateway fee. Then work out the annual figure against your own turnover and compare it to what you pay now.

Where we lose

Five real reasons to buy Epos Now instead.

  1. Integrated card payments. Theirs works; ours does not exist. With us your staff ring up £11.40 and then key £11.40 into your own terminal. On a busy counter that costs seconds and invites mis-keys. Our Dojo integration is blocked on ISV access, which is not a timescale we control, so we will not promise you a date.
  2. Someone who turns up. They have UK phone support and on-site engineers. Nobody from HospitalityApp drives to your pub at 9pm.
  3. The integration catalogue. More than a hundred, against our handful. If you need a hotel PMS, a delivery aggregator or a specific accounting package, check before you assume we connect to it.
  4. Deep stock and recipe costing. Their stock module genuinely goes further than most at this price. Ours does container-first stocktakes and auto-depletion well; it is not a recipe-engineering suite.
  5. Longevity and public evidence. Fifteen years of trading and 26,270 public reviews against a young company with very few. If vendor risk is your main concern, that comparison does not go our way and it would be dishonest to spin it.

And our own answer to the same test. This page tells you to demand a minimum term, a notice period and an exit cost in writing, so here are ours. Monthly is rolling: no minimum term, no notice period, no exit fee, cancelled by you from the billing screen. Annual is twelve months paid up front for two months’ discount and is non-refundable — a real commitment, and we would rather you knew that before choosing it. Price rises come with 30 days’ notice and a penalty-free exit. All of it is in section 6 of our terms. When this page first went up we did not publish any of that, and this paragraph said so. Holding ourselves to the standard is what got it fixed.

If you are switching

What to sort out before you move.

  1. Find both of your end dates. The licence clock and the care plan clock are different, and the care plan is the long one. Ask for both in writing, along with the notice period on each.
  2. Work out the exit cost before you decide. If you are mid-term, the aggregate of remaining payments may make waiting the cheaper option. Get the figure, then choose.
  3. Check who owns your hardware. If you took a £0-upfront deal, the terminal may not be yours to keep, and there is a return window with an invoice attached to missing it.
  4. Sort your card processing separately. It is a separate contract with its own 12-month clock. Moving till and moving acquirer at once is two migrations; you may want them a month apart.
  5. Export your data first. Products, categories, prices, staff, sales history. Do it while you still have an active account, not afterwards.

We have migrated a venue off ICRTouch from a full export, so we are not starting from nothing on this — but be sceptical of anyone, including us, who tells you a till migration is trivial.
