What makes it different

Three things most rota spreadsheets get wrong.

  • Overnight shifts. A shift from 17:00 to 01:00 is eight hours. In a naive spreadsheet it comes out as minus sixteen, so people either fudge it or split the row. This template adds a day whenever the end time is earlier than the start.
  • Labour cost as you build. The Summary sheet divides labour cost by your expected net sales and bands the answer green, amber or red against your target. You find out the week is over budget before you publish it, not after payday.
  • Two Working Time checks. It flags any shift over six hours with under a 20-minute break, and any shift starting less than eleven hours after that person's last one finished. Both are the mistakes a busy manager makes at 5pm on a Thursday.

Six steps

How to use it.

  1. Open it in Excel, Google Sheets, Numbers or LibreOffice, and put Monday's date in cell B3.
  2. Enter one row per shift — date, role, name, start, end. Times go in as 17:00, not as text.
  3. Add unpaid break minutes. Hours and cost calculate themselves.
  4. Add hourly rates. Look up the current National Minimum Wage band for each person's age — the rates change every April, which is why this template ships with the rate column blank rather than a figure that will be wrong by next spring.
  5. On the Summary sheet, type your expected net sales (excluding VAT) and your target labour percentage.
  6. Fix the week before you publish it. Green means you are within two points of target. Red means change something now.

The two checks, explained

What the flags actually mean.

“Needs a 20-min break” appears when a shift is longer than six hours and you have entered less than twenty minutes of break. Under the Working Time Regulations 1998, a worker is entitled to an uninterrupted rest break of twenty minutes when daily working time is more than six hours. In hospitality this is the most-breached rule in the book, usually by accident on a short-staffed Saturday.

“Under 11h rest” appears when someone's next shift starts less than eleven hours after their last one finished. The Regulations provide for eleven consecutive hours of rest in each twenty-four-hour period. The classic breach is the close-then-open — finish at 01:00, back on at 09:00 — which is eight hours and is very easy to roster without noticing.

Both flags are a prompt to look again, not legal advice. Different and stricter limits apply to workers under 18, and some sectors and workforce agreements vary. Check your own obligations.

Being honest about it

When the spreadsheet stops working.

A spreadsheet is genuinely fine for a small team and a steady week. Plenty of good venues run on one for years, and we would rather you used a good one than a bad one.

It stops being fine at a predictable point. A spreadsheet cannot tell your team the rota has changed — that is still a WhatsApp message somebody misses. It cannot stop you rostering a person who booked the week off, because it does not know about the holiday. It cannot tell you what people actually worked versus what you planned, so payday becomes a negotiation. And it cannot let someone pick up a shift you could not fill without you re-sending the file.

When those things start costing real money, that is the point to move to something that does them. Common questions

About the template.

Is it really free?

Yes. No email address, no sign-up, no trial. Download it, change it, share it. We publish it because operators who outgrow a spreadsheet sometimes come looking for software, and we would rather be the people who gave them the spreadsheet.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the .xlsx to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets — formulas, conditional formatting and both checks carry across. It also works in Excel 2016 or newer, Apple Numbers and LibreOffice Calc.

Does it handle shifts past midnight?

Yes, and this is the thing most spreadsheet rotas get wrong. 17:00 to 01:00 is calculated as eight hours, not minus sixteen.

Why is the hourly rate column empty?

Deliberately. National Minimum and National Living Wage rates change every April and vary by age band. A figure baked into a downloadable file is wrong within months, and a wrong wage figure is worse than no figure. Look up the current rate for each person's band.

Can I use it for more than one venue?

Yes — take a copy per venue. It is not built to consolidate several sites into one view; if you need that, it is what the multi-site side of the app is for.

Do I have to credit you?

No. Use it however you like, including commercially, with no attribution.
