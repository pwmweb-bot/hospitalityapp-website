PRACTICAL GUIDES FOR HOSPITALITY

Turn your reservations into a service plan

Connect what you know about arrivals with your table layout, team briefing and service handover.

Hospitality App team · 23 September 2026

Read the pattern, not just the total

Two evenings with the same number of booked guests can feel very different. Review when parties are expected, their sizes and how arrivals are grouped. A cluster of bookings at one time puts a different demand on the host, floor and kitchen from a more spread-out service.

Use the reservation list as a starting point for a conversation. Identify the parts of the evening that need preparation rather than waiting for the first queue at the door.

Look at the room before assigning tables

Review the actual layout and the tables available for that service. Consider any planned changes, joined tables or spaces that are not in use. Keep the plan understandable to somebody stepping in to cover the host.

Where guests have shared seating requirements, make sure the team that needs to act on them can find the relevant information. Do not collect extra personal details just because there is somewhere to type them.

Brief the team on what matters

A short pre-service briefing should explain the arrival pattern, larger parties and anything that changes the normal flow. Agree who handles arrivals and who makes the call when a table assignment needs to change.

Keep the briefing focused on decisions. Reading every booking aloud is less helpful than pointing out the few moments that need the team to coordinate.

Keep the floor and kitchen connected

Reservations describe the plan; service shows what is actually happening. Agree how the host, waiting team and kitchen communicate when arrivals bunch together or a party is delayed.

Table-side ordering and kitchen displays can support the flow of orders, but the team still needs a clear way to handle an exception. Decide who owns the next action so an update does not stop at “someone has been told”.

Finish with a useful handover

At the end of service, record the operational lessons that will help the next shift. Was a table arrangement awkward? Did several arrivals repeatedly land together? Was an important update hard to find?

Use those observations to adjust the next briefing or layout. Keep notes specific and relevant to running the venue. The goal is a better next service, not a growing collection of commentary about guests.

FOR YOUR NEXT SHIFT

A short checklist

  • Review arrival times and party sizes.
  • Check the tables available for the service.
  • Agree who owns seating changes and exceptions.
  • Brief the team on the moments that need coordination.
