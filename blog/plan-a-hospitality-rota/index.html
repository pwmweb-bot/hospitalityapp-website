A better rota starts before you assign a shift

A practical way to plan coverage, handle availability and publish a rota your team can work with.

Start with the work, then add the names

A rota is a plan for how a shift will run. Before choosing who works on Friday, list the jobs Friday needs to cover: opening, preparation, counter or table service, breaks, handover and closing. This makes it easier to spot a gap that a headcount alone can hide.

Use a typical week as a starting point, then mark the exceptions. A function, delivery, sports fixture or large booking can change the work even when the opening hours stay the same.

Check availability before filling the grid

Gather approved leave, known unavailability and any changes to roles before you allocate shifts. Agree a clear point in the week for staff to submit requests, and explain how late changes should be raised.

Keep the information in one place. If availability is spread across private messages, a notebook and a manager’s memory, the rota becomes an exercise in finding the latest version.

Check the shape of each shift

Look at the shift as a sequence. Who opens? Who is ready when demand builds? Who covers a break? Who stays for the handover and close? Make sure the skills you need are present, rather than assuming that any available person can cover any role.

Review planned hours and cost alongside the operational picture. A quiet period can still contain preparation and cleaning work. Avoid treating the sales forecast as the only thing the team has to deliver.

Give changes a clear route

Publish to a single place and tell the team when the rota is ready. Decide who approves swaps, how cover is requested and when a change becomes final. A proposed swap should not leave two people with different expectations about who is turning up.

When something changes, update the rota itself. A message can explain the change, but it should not become a competing version of the plan.

Use the last week to improve the next one

After the week, compare the plan with the hours recorded. Ask managers where they felt stretched and where a handover was awkward. Look for repeated problems before changing a template.

Choose one improvement for the next rota: a better overlap, a clearer opening role or an earlier availability deadline. A useful review produces a specific change, not simply a judgement that the week was busy.

FOR YOUR NEXT SHIFT

A short checklist

  • List the work each shift must cover.
  • Check leave, availability and role coverage.
  • Review handovers, breaks and planned cost.
  • Publish one version and define the change process.
