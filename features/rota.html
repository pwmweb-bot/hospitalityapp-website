What it does

Everything that goes into building a week.

  • Week-by-week builder with drag-and-drop assignment.
  • Four views — by Role, by Staff, Timeline and Week/Day/List. Different jobs need different shapes: "have I got a chef on Friday?" is a role question; "is Sam over their hours?" is a staff question; "who overlaps at 7pm?" is a timeline question. View choice persists per screen width.
  • Live labour-cost ticker — running £ and labour % update as you drag.
  • Over-budget guard on publish — a confirmation dialog if the week exceeds your target % of expected takings. A deliberate speed bump on the most expensive mistake in the business.
  • Publish — one button makes the week real and push-notifies everyone rostered. Staff never see a draft.
  • Unassigned / open shifts — leave a shift unfilled and it goes into a pool staff swipe-right to claim on the phone.
  • Offer a shift — broadcast to everyone or target named people. First accept wins, race-safe.
  • Double-booking guard — warns (or hard-blocks, per venue policy) when someone is rostered elsewhere. Essential once staff work at more than one of your sites.
  • Leave-aware conflicts — a shift whose assignee is on approved leave or off sick is stripe-flagged NEEDS COVER / OUT SICK instead of quietly sitting there.
  • Find cover (native app) — one tap reassigns, with the list already marked Available / On leave / Off sick / Already scheduled.
  • Shift templates — named patterns ("Fri Late Bar", 17:00–01:00, Bar staff) carrying a briefing note and a task list.
  • Weekly templates — a named bundle of shift templates ("Standard week", "Weekend cover") applied to a whole week in one action. Idempotent, so re-applying doesn't duplicate.
  • Per-shift task lists phased Opening / Mid-shift / Closing, optionally requiring a photo, optionally linked to a compliance check so ticking it counts twice.
  • Copy previous week — most weeks look like last week.
  • Team rota visibility toggle — per venue: staff see the whole team's week or only their own shifts.

Staff self-service: shift swaps (request a named colleague, they accept or decline with a reason, a manager approves), time off with part-day portions (first day / last day / custom hours), and an availability calendar (one-off, weekly recurring, or a date range).

Why it matters

The commercial argument for owning your rota.

An independent operator's edge over a chain is speed of decision. On the rota, that means knowing labour cost as you build, not two weeks after the shifts have been worked. Every shift carries an hourly rate; the running total is what a chain's ops director looks at, in real time, on your kitchen counter.

The over-budget guard is where that number lands hardest. Hitting Publish and seeing "This week is £180 over your labour target" one Wednesday changes how you build the next one. Nobody argues with a number they saw before they clicked.

The template and copy-last-week machinery is a productivity floor: building a rota should be five minutes of exceptions, not an hour of blank-page dread. Shift templates carrying task lists mean the opening checklist, mid-shift jobs and closedown tick list ship with the shift — so compliance rides on work that was going to happen anyway.

The joins are the product — anyone can sell a rota. Here's what plugs into this one.

Time & attendance

Rota shifts become the expected pattern; clock punches reconcile against them. A forgotten clock-out doesn't become a 30-hour shift.

Compliance & tasks
