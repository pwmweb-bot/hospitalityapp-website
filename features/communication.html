What it does

Six things that stop the group chat.

  • Notice board — announcements, reminders and urgent notices, pinnable, with read receipts. The thing that currently lives in a WhatsApp group nobody reads.
  • Direct messages — 1:1 chat in-app. In v1 staff message managers and the owner, not each other — deliberate.
  • Contacts directory — suppliers, maintenance, utilities, property, emergency. Tap to dial. Up to three emergency contacts pin to a banner — the number you need at 11pm when the cellar floods is one tap from any screen.
  • Push notifications with four opt-out categories: Shifts & rota, Requests & approvals, Messages & notices, Tasks & compliance. Preferences are honoured server-side before a push is sent — an opt-out is real, not cosmetic.
  • Shift reminders — automatic: 2 hours before a shift, a missed clock-in alert (to the staff member and their managers), and a clock-out nudge.
  • In-app help — 22 persona-aware topics with related links and a support CTA.
  • Support tickets — raised from inside the app, landing in the operator console.
Why it matters

The three things WhatsApp can't do.

The first is acknowledge-required notices. A pinned noticeboard post with read receipts is the evidence that everyone saw the allergen briefing, the new cellar procedure, the change to opening hours. A WhatsApp thumbs-up is not.

The second is server-side opt-outs. If a staff member turns off "Messages & notices" at midnight, that isn't advice to the app — the notification service doesn't queue or deliver it. There's no back-door route where a manager can override or "just this once" it.

The third is the emergency banner. A supplier list is only useful when you can find the number in a hurry. Pinning up to three to a persistent banner means the cellar-flooded, gas-cut-off, alarm-going-off number is one tap from wherever you happened to be in the app.
