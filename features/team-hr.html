What it does

Everything on a person, in one place.

  • Roster with All / On today / On leave filters.
  • Invite by email — a branded link the invitee taps to set a password and land in the app. Backup link, resend for expired invites, PENDING badge in the rota picker.
  • The full HR record: name, contact, role, hourly rate, start/end date, contract type, NI number, tax code, right-to-work (document type, share code, verified-on date, "Not verified" warning), DOB (drives NMW age bands), address, emergency contact, bank details for payroll.
  • Clock-in code and till PIN per person.
  • Per-area permissions — view/edit/approve toggles across Menu, Staff, Rota, Time off, Notice board, Shift swaps.
  • Works at other venues — multi-site staff with a per-venue hourly rate.
  • Deactivate a leaver without deleting anything — history, punches and audit remain intact.
Why it matters

The two things that cost real money at inspection.

Right-to-work checks and National Minimum Wage banding are the two areas HMRC and Immigration Enforcement care most about. A missing share-code note or a birthday that's silently crossed a NMW threshold are the fines you hear about later. Holding the HR record next to the rota — where age is already the reason someone gets £11.44 rather than £8.60 — is the loop that closes.

The other piece is column-level privacy enforced in the database. Pay and HR data sits behind a view and a write-guard trigger, so an escalation attempt or a tampered request hits the database's answer, not the front end's. It's the difference between "we hid it in the UI" and "they can't reach it".

How it joins to the rest

The staff record is a spine.

Rota & scheduling

Role chips, hourly rates, availability, holiday balances — all read from the same record. Multi-site staff carry per-venue rates.

Payroll & pay

NI, tax code and bank details live here; the pay run pulls them without transcription.

Compliance & tasks

Workforce compliance (right-to-work, DBS, training expiry) is populated from the staff record. Two views, one truth.
