What it does

Everything the clock tracks and enforces.

  • Three clock-in methods, combinable — GPS geofence, QR code, NFC tag. Choose the friction that fits.
  • Printable QR poster — generated in-app with staff instructions and a big code. Rotate the secret any time and reprint.
  • NFC tag registry — register tags by UID with a label ("Bar till"). Remove a lost one instantly.
  • Overnight-safe — a shift crossing midnight can still be clocked out at 02:00.
  • Clock-out grace window — closers aren't punished for finishing 20 minutes late.
  • Ad-hoc clock-in — Everyone / Managers only / Off, per venue. Worked time is never invisible.
  • Auto-close sweep — a forgotten clock-out doesn't become a 30-hour shift.
  • Future-punch clamp — a device with a wrong clock can't write a punch into the future.
  • Discrepancies queue — every no-show, still-clocked-in and flagged session lands in one manager screen with real resolutions: Pay as planned, Close out at 23:15, Mark as no-show, Confirm as-is, Dismiss — each with an audit note.
Why it matters

The commercial difference the discrepancies queue makes.

The queue is the difference between payroll being a two-hour reconciliation and a five-minute review. Every exception is surfaced with the evidence and a one-tap resolution, and nothing silently defaults to "pay it" — that's the failure mode of a lot of "modern" time-and-attendance products: they hide the messy sessions so the run doesn't stall, and the wage bill is quietly higher than it should be.

The clock model is immutable by design. Punches are append-only time_entries; sessions are derived by a database trigger. You can correct a session; you cannot rewrite history without leaving a trace. That's what makes the pay conversation a data conversation, not a memory conversation.
