WHAT IT DOES

Six things that
stop the group chat.

  • A notice board for announcements, reminders and urgent notices — pinnable, with read receipts. The thing that currently lives in a group chat nobody reads.
  • Direct messages in the app. Staff message managers and the owner rather than each other, which is deliberate.
  • A contacts directory — suppliers, maintenance, utilities, property, emergency, tap to dial. Up to three emergency contacts pin to a banner, so the number you need at 11pm when the cellar floods is one tap from any screen.
  • Push notifications with four opt-out categories — shifts and rota, requests and approvals, messages and notices, tasks and compliance. Preferences are honoured on the server before a push is sent, so an opt-out is real rather than cosmetic.
  • Automatic shift reminders — two hours before a shift, a missed clock-in alert to the staff member and their managers, and a clock-out nudge.
  • In-app help and support tickets — persona-aware topics with related links, and a ticket raised from inside the app.

WHY IT MATTERS

The three things
a group chat cannot do.

The first is a notice that has to be acknowledged. A pinned post with read receipts is the evidence that everyone saw the allergen briefing, the new cellar procedure, the change to opening hours. A thumbs-up in a chat thread is not.

The second is a real opt-out. If a staff member turns off messages and notices at midnight, that is not advice to the app — the notification service does not queue it or deliver it, and there is no back-door route where a manager can override it just this once.

The third is the emergency banner. A supplier list is only useful when you can find the number in a hurry. Pinning three of them to a persistent banner means the cellar-flooded, gas-cut-off, alarm-going-off number is one tap from wherever you happened to be.
