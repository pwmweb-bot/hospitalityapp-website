What it does

Stock, ordered and counted the way a cellar works.

  • Stock items with unit, cost, supplier, reorder level, par level, reorder quantity.
  • Link a stock item to a till product and it auto-depletes as the till sells — set units-used per sale and container size, and the system does the arithmetic.
  • To order — everything at or under its reorder level, grouped by supplier, ready to send.
  • Stocktake — snapshot, count, see live variance, then apply to reconcile (or cancel).
  • Movements — a full, dated audit log: receive, waste / spillage, adjustment — each with a note.
  • On the phone: add a product and run a stocktake container-first — kegs and bottles counted with a stepper and 0 · ¼ · ½ · ¾ · Full chips. Never millilitres. Stocktakes are resumable, grouped by area (Cellar, Back bar, Kitchen).
Why it matters

GP stops being a story.

Most independents count stock every quarter, plug the numbers into a spreadsheet, get a GP figure they don't quite believe, and shelve it until next quarter. Nothing changes. When stock auto-depletes on sale and waste is recorded where it happens, variance is visible the day of the count — and the Profit tab in Insights has real costs behind it.

The container-first counting model is why staff actually finish a stocktake. Counting quarters is a job you can do in an hour; counting millilitres is a job you never start.

How it joins to the rest

Stock is downstream of the till and upstream of Profit.

Till / POS

Linked stock items auto-deplete on every sale. Container costs on the product; the till does the arithmetic.

Reporting & insights

Profit tab: revenue, GP, GP%, stock at cost, waste this period, GP by category. Real numbers, not stories.
