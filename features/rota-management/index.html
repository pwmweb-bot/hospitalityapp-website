WHAT IT DOES

Everything that goes into
building a week.

  • Week-by-week builder with drag-and-drop assignment, and a live labour-cost ticker — running £ and labour % update as you drag, so you find out you have over-rostered while building, not on payday.
  • Four views — by role, by staff, timeline, and week/day/list. Different jobs need different shapes: “have I got a chef on Friday?” is a role question, “is Sam over their hours?” is a staff question, “who overlaps at 7pm?” is a timeline question. The choice persists per screen width.
  • Over-budget guard on publish — a confirmation if the week exceeds your target percentage of expected takings. A deliberate speed bump on the most expensive mistake in the business.
  • Publish makes the week real and notifies everyone rostered. Staff never see a draft.
  • Open shifts — leave a shift unfilled and it goes into a pool staff claim from their phone. Or offer a shift to everyone or to named people; first accept wins, race-safe.
  • Double-booking guard warns, or hard-blocks per venue policy, when someone is already rostered elsewhere. Essential once staff work at more than one of your sites.
  • Leave-aware conflicts — a shift whose assignee is on approved leave or off sick is flagged needs cover rather than quietly sitting there, and find cover reassigns in one tap from a list already marked available, on leave, off sick or already scheduled.
  • Shift and weekly templates — named patterns carrying a briefing note and a task list, bundled into a named week you apply in one action. Re-applying does not duplicate.
  • Per-shift task lists phased opening, mid-shift and closing, optionally requiring a photo, optionally linked to a compliance check so ticking it counts twice.
  • Copy previous week, because most weeks look like last week. And a per-venue toggle for whether staff see the whole team’s week or only their own shifts.
  • Staff self-service — shift swaps with a named colleague and manager approval, time off with part-day portions, and an availability calendar (one-off, weekly recurring, or a date range).

WHY IT MATTERS

Knowing labour cost
as you build it.

An independent operator’s edge over a chain is speed of decision. On the rota that means knowing labour cost as you build, not two weeks after the shifts have been worked. Every shift carries an hourly rate, and the running total is the number a chain’s ops director watches in real time — here it is on your kitchen counter.

The over-budget guard is where that number lands hardest. Hitting publish and seeing that the week is £180 over your labour target, on a Wednesday, changes how you build the next one. Nobody argues with a number they saw before they clicked.

The template and copy-last-week machinery is a productivity floor: building a rota should be five minutes of exceptions, not an hour of blank-page dread. Shift templates that carry task lists mean the opening checklist and the closedown list ship with the shift, so compliance rides on work that was going to happen anyway.
