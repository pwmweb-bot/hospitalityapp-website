What it does

Events, calendar, venue setup, devices.

  • Events — private parties, functions, sports fixtures, holidays. Each carries a status, expected covers, location and brief, plus its own task list and discussion thread.
  • Events on the rota — inline on the day they fall on, so whoever's building the week can see the function.
  • Calendar — a month grid of events, approved time off and sick leave. Deliberately not shifts — that's the rota's job.
  • Venue setup — profile, address, opening hours, logo, cover image, business type, roles and custom positions, "serves food" (which unlocks food-specific compliance defaults), and labour benchmarks (target labour % and weekly labour budget — what makes every labour figure elsewhere mean something).
  • Devices — pair a tablet or phone with a single-use code, no login. Three modes: Till, Kitchen display (pinned to a station or the whole pass), Mobile ordering. Name it, see last-seen, rename, revoke. A lost tablet is revoked from a phone. Includes a native ESC/POS receipt-print bridge over the venue LAN (port 9100).
  • White-label branding — upload a logo and set one brand colour, and it recolours the app, the till and the receipts.
Why it matters

The Saturday function nobody remembered.

Independent venues live and die by the ability to see a function in the week's shape. A 60-cover birthday on Saturday isn't a Saturday — it's four extra shifts, an ordered ice run, a temporary rearrangement of the floor plan, and a chef who needs to know the day before. Putting the event on the rota, with a task list and a discussion thread the shift can see, means the function gets planned by whoever is building the week — not by a manager doing a check on Friday evening.

Device mode is the other quiet enabler. A till that requires a login is a till that gets left logged in as whoever opened. Claim-scoped device sessions mean the tablet is trusted, the person is identified by their PIN at the point of sale, and a lost tablet is revoked from a phone.
