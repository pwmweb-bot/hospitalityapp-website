What it does

Ordering without the walk back to the till.

  • Paired phone as a mobile till — one code, no login, boots straight into ordering.
  • PIN attribution — the next order is attributed to whoever unlocked the phone. Every ticket has a name.
  • Three layouts to suit the menu — Category tabs, Browse & drill, One long scroll — plus descriptor and compact-row toggles.
  • Same pipeline as the tablet till — line notes, tips, table + tab handling, allergen notes, kitchen station routing.
  • Race-safe pairing — a lost or stolen phone can be revoked from another paired device, immediately.
Why it matters

Where the covers per server come from.

The reason a phone-as-a-till pays for itself isn't feature-count — it's that the round trip to the tablet till stops happening. On a Friday-night rush that's ten to twenty walks per hour per server. The kitchen gets the ticket thirty seconds earlier; the drink gets to the table two minutes sooner; nobody is queued behind the person paying for their round.

The trick isn't the phone. Phones have been ordering-capable for years. The trick is a PIN-unlock model that lets the phone belong to the venue, not the server — so one device covers a shift instead of one-per-person, and the shrinkage conversation is still "who's PIN?", not "whose phone?".
