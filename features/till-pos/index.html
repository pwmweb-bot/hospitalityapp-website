WHAT IT DOES

A till built for
the way a bar sells.

  • Counter service or table service, set per venue. A category rail, sub-category chips and search, arranged the way the bar actually sells.
  • The measure popup — half or pint, single or double, 175ml, 250ml or bottle. Each measure carries its own price and its own pour size, so gross profit is calculated per pour rather than per product. A line can carry an option, an extra, or a mixer.
  • Line notes, void line, running subtotal, service charge and total, and a note that prints on both the bill and the kitchen ticket. Tap a dish twice and it sits on the bill twice, so each plate can have its own note. Drinks still stack.
  • Payments, tips and splits. Split the bill by item. Tips can be card or cash, and you can split a tip. A discount never changes the tip. You can also adjust a price in pounds, and record why.
  • Bar tabs saved against a named tab, with open count, outstanding and taken-today on the tab list. Tables with a floor plan, seat covers, a food-ready pill and pay-per-table. Pay at the table by handing the tablet over, or send a payment link.
  • Print the bill. Receipts can be printed, reprinted, previewed or emailed. Set how big the words are on the printed receipt. Put your logo on it. Daily takings sync back automatically, so the takings record fills itself.
  • A lock screen with PIN and idle auto-lock. The next order is attributed to whoever unlocked it, so every sale has a name against it. A held sale stays with that PIN.
  • Refunds, managers only. Refund one item, or the whole sale, whether it was cash, card or Apple Pay. A REFUND receipt prints, and the reason is recorded.
  • Staff drinks have their own button, recorded against a person. No-sale drawer opens are logged, counted and reported.
  • Back office — categories, products and measures, each with its own price, pour size and cost; VAT; kitchen station; tables and zones. Set the venue colours, the logos, and the logo on the receipt. An allergen grid, and a chart you can print for a guest.
  • Printers. Food can go to the kitchen and drinks to the bar. Test a printer without standing next to it.
  • The day. Count the drawer at the start. At the end, record the drawer and the card-machine total. The difference is on the Z, and you can reprint a Z.

WHY IT MATTERS

What separates a hospitality till
from a retail one.

Two things, and only two. The first is the measure popup. A pint and a half share a product; a single and a double share a spirit; a 175 and a 250 share a wine bottle. If those are not the same product at different prices, gross profit and stock depletion are theatre.

The second is the model of trust. Every sale carries the name of the person who took it, because the next order is not served until someone re-enters their PIN. That is what turns drawer opens, refunds and staff drinks from arguments into numbers on a report.

There is no per-terminal fee, no processor lock-in and no point-of-sale bolt-on. We do not earn a rebate from a card processor, so we have no reason to bundle one — your card rate stays yours to negotiate.

GOOD TO KNOW

A few practical answers.

Do I need Complete Plus for a till?

No. Till / POS is included in Complete. Complete Plus adds mobile ordering, kitchen display, reservations and floor plans.

Can I use Windows?

Yes. Hospitality App Till for Windows runs the same till on a Windows 10 or 11 terminal, or on a PC you already have.

Can a bar tab go on a room bill?

Yes, where room management is switched on. Take the order at the bar, or at the table on a staff phone. From that open tab, charge it to the room. It lands on the folio, and the guest pays everything at check-out. See easy room management.
