What it does

Ten categories, one habit.

  • Ten categories: Food safety, Fire safety, Alcohol licensing, Health & safety, Workforce, Background checks, Data protection, Environmental, Accessibility, Insurance.
  • Seed starter checks for a category in one tap — you're not staring at a blank page.
  • Define a check with a frequency (daily → annually, or on-event), an optional numeric reading with unit and min/max band, required photo evidence, and a renewal/expiry date with a reminder lead time.
  • Log a check with reading, notes and a photo. Out-of-range readings are highlighted, not silently accepted.
  • Documents — upload certificates and policies with expiry dates, scoped to a check or a whole category, with EXPIRED badges.
  • Daily tasks — recurring venue-wide or role-scoped jobs with time slots, weekday selection, readings and photos. Staff see them as a tick list on Today with a team tally.
  • Shift checklists — tasks that ride on the shift, phased opening/during/closing. Missed closing tasks surface in the manager's inbox.
  • Daily email digest of overdue and due-soon checks to the owner and every active manager.
Why it matters

Compliance that lives inside the work gets done.

The trick isn't the check screen. Every compliance product has one. The trick is attaching the check to a shift task — so opening the walk-in at 07:00 and logging its temperature at 07:01 is a single job on a checklist that had to be done anyway. Compliance that lives inside the work gets done; compliance in a separate folder does not.

Expiring-certificate reminders are the other quiet win. Public liability insurance, food-hygiene certificates, EHO ratings — the £4,000 renewal you forgot until the day it lapsed is the one that costs you. The daily digest, and the reminder lead time on each document, are the reason it stops happening.
