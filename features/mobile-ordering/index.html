WHAT IT DOES

Ordering without
the walk back to the till.

  • A paired phone as a mobile till — one code, no login, straight into ordering.
  • PIN attribution. The next order belongs to whoever unlocked the phone, so every ticket has a name on it.
  • Three layouts to suit the menu — category tabs, browse and drill, or one long scroll — plus descriptor and compact-row toggles.
  • The same pipeline as the tablet till — line notes, tips, tables and tabs, allergen notes, and kitchen station routing.
  • Race-safe pairing. A lost or stolen phone is revoked from another paired device, immediately.

WHY IT MATTERS

Where the extra covers
per server come from.

The reason a phone-as-a-till pays for itself is not the feature count. It is that the round trip to the tablet till stops happening. On a Friday-night rush that is ten to twenty walks an hour per server. The kitchen gets the ticket thirty seconds earlier, the drink reaches the table two minutes sooner, and nobody is queued behind the person paying for their round.

The trick is not the phone; phones have been ordering-capable for years. The trick is a PIN-unlock model that lets the phone belong to the venue rather than to the server — so one device covers a shift instead of one per person, and the shrinkage conversation is still about whose PIN it was, not whose phone.
